# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Activity Team members at a Thai organization. They use this daily to coordinate speakers across multiple seminars — sending invitation emails, chasing agendas, tracking responses, and handling last-minute issues. Context is operational: desk-based, often managing several speakers at once across different deadline states. They are not technical. Speed and clarity matter; they don't have time to learn a tool.

## Product Purpose

Internal speaker CRM. Manages the full speaker pipeline from initial contact (pending) through confirmed, agenda submitted, and completed — with side channels for AI-drafted emails, calendar invites, agenda file uploads via a speaker-facing portal, and a support ticket system. Success looks like zero missed deadlines and a team that never has to wonder what state a speaker is in.

## Brand Personality

Precise, structured, professional. The tool should feel like a well-organized colleague — calm under pressure, information always at hand, no unnecessary words. Voice is concise and direct in both Thai and English.

## Anti-references

- Not hyper-casual or consumer-app: no excessive emoji, bubbly illustration, playful microinteractions, or consumer-product softness. This is a work tool.
- Not generic Thai government or legacy enterprise admin: no dense gray tables, inconsistent type, or cluttered form-heavy layouts.

## Design Principles

1. **Status is the product** — The pipeline state (pending → confirmed → completed) is the most important information on every screen. Make it the most scannable element, not an afterthought.
2. **Task clarity over decoration** — Every visual element must reduce friction or communicate information. If it does neither, remove it.
3. **Information density without overwhelm** — Show what matters at the right moment. Defer secondary detail to modals and drawers; the list view must breathe.
4. **Calm precision** — Spacious layouts, clean typographic hierarchy, no noise. The team is under deadline pressure; the tool should feel like a steadying hand.
5. **Language-first** — Thai copy is a first-class design decision. Labels, status names, and error messages must be as carefully considered as any layout choice.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Bilingual Thai/English UI — Thai is primary. Keyboard navigable. Sufficient contrast on all text including status badges and placeholder text. No reduced-motion default required, but all animations must have a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback.
